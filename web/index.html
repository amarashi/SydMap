SYDMAPSydney

Every building standing today, by age and historical context.

About this map

All 30,000 buildings of the City of Sydney and North Sydney, either side of the Harbour Bridge, coloured by the year they were built and set in the history that shaped them.

A non-commercial project, made out of curiosity and for education.

SydMap acknowledges the Gadigal people of the Eora Nation, the Traditional Custodians of the land the City of Sydney stands on, and the Cammeraygal people, the Traditional Custodians of the land of North Sydney. Sovereignty was never ceded.

A Sydney rebuild of LAYRS: Amsterdam by Alex Barakov (MIT licence), itself inspired by Sasha Bogachev's Paris, building by building. Its look was read off the Amsterdam page with designlang; the 2D view runs RustGIS.

Open source

The code and the data pipeline are on GitHub. Know a piece of Sydney's history that lives in its buildings? Add it as a layer: a list of buildings, a short story for each and the source it rests on.

How old is each building?

Sydney, unlike Amsterdam, has no register that dates every building. Each one takes the best evidence there is, and its card says which:

  • Documented (–): a year from Wikidata, from the lead of its Wikipedia article (for heritage-listed places, written from the NSW State Heritage Inventory) or from OpenStreetMap.
  • Strata plan (–): apartment buildings are strata-titled when they are finished, so the year their strata plan was registered dates them. Plans since 1994 carry their date in the NSW cadastre; older ones are placed by their number between those and 1961, when strata title began.
  • Heritage area (–): buildings the City of Sydney marks as contributing to a heritage conservation area belong to its historic fabric; they are dated from the documented heritage buildings around them.
  • Neighbours (–): the rest take the years of the dated buildings around them of similar height. Towers over 46 m, Sydney's height limit until 1957, only take years after it.

Estimated years are paler on the map and marked “c.” on the cards, with the range they come from. Treat them as an educated guess about the period, not as a date.

Data

Downloaded in October 2026.

  • Footprints: Overture Maps (release 2026-09-23), from OpenStreetMap and Microsoft's machine-learned footprints. ODbL; © OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Heights: OpenStreetMap heights and levels; elsewhere GlobalBuildingAtlas (Zhu et al., TU Munich, 2025), modelled from satellite imagery, CC BY-NC 4.0: one more reason this map is non-commercial. The 2D GIS download leaves these modelled heights out.
  • Boundaries and strata plans: NSW Spatial Services. CC BY 4.0.
  • Heritage: the State Heritage Register (Heritage NSW) and the local environmental plans' heritage maps (NSW Department of Planning), CC BY 4.0; heritage conservation area contributions from the City of Sydney's Development Control Plan 2012, CC BY 4.0.
  • History layers: City of Sydney open data (Aboriginal history places from Barani; Developing Sydney 1900–1920 from the City of Sydney Archives), CC BY 4.0; OpenStreetMap, ODbL.
  • Names, photos, stories: Wikidata (CC0), Wikipedia (CC BY-SA) and Wikimedia Commons; each photo credits its author and licence.
