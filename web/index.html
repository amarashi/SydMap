Sydney

SydMap

    Notes

    Sydney has never kept a register of when its buildings went up. SydMap pieces one together, building by building, from open data.

    What you are looking at

    All 135,843 buildings around the harbour as they stand today, each coloured by the age of the city it belongs to, from the convict town to Barangaroo. They cover both shores of the harbour and the Parramatta River, from Putney and Concord to Manly, and the ocean coast from Bondi to Coogee: thirteen councils, some whole and some in part (the downloads below list them). Move along the line at the bottom to see the city in any year, or press play to watch it go up.

    Getting around

    Drag the city to turn it round. To slide it along, drag with the right button or hold Shift; to come closer, scroll or pinch. Move the sun along the line, or press play, to change the year; choose a building to read about it.

    How each year is known

    Every building carries the best evidence there is, and its card says which.

    Documented –
    A year from Wikidata, from the opening of its Wikipedia article (for heritage-listed places, written from the NSW State Heritage Inventory) or from OpenStreetMap.
    Strata plan –
    Apartment buildings are strata-titled when they are finished, so the year their plan was registered dates them. Plans since 1994 carry their date in the NSW cadastre; older ones are placed by their number, back to 1961, when strata title began.
    Heritage –
    The other buildings of a State Heritage Register listing take the listing's years. Buildings the City marks as part of a heritage area's historic fabric, and low-rise buildings in the other councils' heritage areas, are dated from the documented heritage buildings around them.
    Neighbours –
    The rest take the years of the dated buildings around them of a similar height. Towers over 46 m, Sydney's height limit until 1957, only take later years. Houses take documented years; where the map does not know a building is a house, a low-rise building in the suburbs also takes the years of the heritage areas, villas and townhouses around it.

    Estimated years are paler on the map and marked “c.” on the cards, with the range they come from. Read them as a period, not a date.

    Heights

    From OpenStreetMap where it has them, otherwise modelled from satellite imagery by GlobalBuildingAtlas, or assumed to be two storeys. Storeys are counted where they are mapped and otherwise worked out at 3.2 m a floor.

    Take the data with you

    Where the data comes from

    Downloaded in October 2026.

    • Footprints Overture Maps (2026-09-23), from OpenStreetMap and Microsoft's machine-learned footprints. ODbL, © OpenStreetMap contributors.
    • Heights OpenStreetMap; elsewhere GlobalBuildingAtlas (Zhu et al., TU Munich, 2025), CC BY-NC 4.0, one more reason SydMap is non-commercial. The downloads leave these modelled heights out.
    • Landmarks The Opera House's shells follow the spherical geometry they were built to, as Ove Arup & Partners' Michael Lewis describes it (Royal Society of NSW, 1973). The Harbour Bridge is built from its published figures: two 28-panel arch trusses, a 504 m span, a 134 m summit.
    • Boundaries and strata plans NSW Spatial Services, CC BY 4.0.
    • Heritage The State Heritage Register (Heritage NSW) and the local environmental plans' heritage maps (NSW Department of Planning), CC BY 4.0; heritage area contributions from the City of Sydney's Development Control Plan 2012, CC BY 4.0.
    • Stories City of Sydney open data (Aboriginal history places from Barani; Developing Sydney 1900–1920 from the City of Sydney Archives), CC BY 4.0; OpenStreetMap, ODbL.
    • Names, photos, histories Wikidata (CC0), Wikipedia (CC BY-SA) and Wikimedia Commons; each photo credits its author and licence.

    Add to it

    The code and the data pipeline are open on GitHub. Know a piece of Sydney's history that lives in its buildings? Add it as a story: a list of buildings, a few lines on each, and the source it rests on.

    The original idea

    SydMap rebuilds LAYRS: Amsterdam by Alex Barakov (MIT licence), itself inspired by Alexander Bogachev's Paris, building by building, for Sydney and from Sydney's own open data.
